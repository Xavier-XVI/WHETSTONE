The Problem

Most agents get built without their builder ever stating the job — cleanly, out loud, in one sentence.

You know roughly what you want it to do. But "roughly" is the whole problem. If you can't say what the job is, what it takes in, and what it must refuse, the agent has nothing firm to hold to — so it goes loose in every direction. It answers inputs it was never meant for. It drifts wider than you intended. It makes the calls you never made, because you left the space open for it to make them.

THE TRAP FOR CAPABLE BUILDERS

You understand the job so completely that you never write it down. It's obvious — to you. So the folder gets built on an intent that can never be checked, because there's nothing stated to check it against.

Ask a model to "review my agent" and it critiques your folder against a job it invented for you. Most tools go further and rewrite — handing back a tidier folder built on that same invented job. Now you're shipping someone else's guess about what your agent is for.

How WHETSTONE Works

It refuses to read your folder for critique until you've stated — and defended — the job.

01 · Anchor
The job comes first

State what the agent does, what it takes in, what it must refuse. WHETSTONE pressure-tests it — one job, or three? — then anchors every finding to your words, never its own inference.

02 · Prosecute
Six lenses

A deterministic pass for the facts, then a read through six lenses — job clarity, scope, enforcement, fidelity, references, handoff — hunting every place the folder contradicts the stated job.

03 · Sort
Defect vs. decision

One honest test: can this be resolved from the folder and the job alone? Yes — a defect to just fix. No — a decision only your knowledge of your users and domain can settle.

04 · Illustrate
The vital few forks

It surfaces the three-to-five decisions that most threaten the job, each shown on a real input from your agent's domain, both branches sketched with their cost named — then it stops.

05 · Log
Located and anchored

Every finding cites a file and line and quotes it, and names how it hurts the job. The whole run is logged verbatim and dated — proof of work you can revisit.

06 · Close
A script says done

WHETSTONE can't declare a review complete on its own say-so. A closure script does — and only when no high-impact decision is left open.

What Changes

The shift isn't that your agent gets fixed. It's that you finally know what it's for — and where you never decided.

Before

The job lives in your head. The folder reads fine because you supply the missing meaning as you go. The gaps stay invisible until an output goes sideways in a way you can't trace back to a line you wrote.

After

The job is on the record, in your own words, pressure-tested. Weak spots are located and anchored. Cheap problems are sorted out as defects. The real forks you'd been resolving by accident are laid in front of you as explicit choices.

Decisions made on purpose, not by default. The success signal WHETSTONE holds itself to is surfacing at least one decision you hadn't realized you'd made. And because it refuses to rewrite your files, the sharper judgment stays yours — the next agent you build starts from a tighter job.

What WHETSTONE Is Not

  • ✕Not a rewriter. It illustrates what a choice would do; it never authors your fix. An editor that hands you a fixed version taught you nothing.
  • ✕Not a grader. No score, no rubric, no leaderboard.
  • ✕Not a general reviewer. It reviews single-specialist ICM folders — not multi-folder agencies or non-ICM agents. Handed one, it says so and stops.
  • ✕Not a decision-maker. It makes your decisions cheap to make, then leaves them to you.
